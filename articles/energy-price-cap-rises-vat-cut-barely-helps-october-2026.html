Bills Go Up Again: The Price Cap Is Now £1,723 — and the VAT Cut Only Cushions It

From 1 October the typical household bill rose from £1,663 to £1,723 a year. The government's VAT cut on electricity stopped it being worse, but gas bills are up 8% and the funding for the cut has been questioned. Here is what is really going on.

Cutting VAT on electricity was always going to be sold as the answer to the cost-of-living crisis. Winter is coming, and the price cap has gone up anyway.

What Ofgem Announced

Ofgem confirmed on 26 August that the energy price cap would rise by 4% from 1 October to 31 December 2026. For a typical household using both gas and electricity, that takes the bill from £1,663 to £1,723 a year, up £60 or about £5 a month. Gas bills rise by 8%. Households with no gas see a rise of less than 1%.

In fairness, Ofgem says about 35% of households, around 11 million, are on fixed tariffs and are not affected by this change.

The VAT Cut: Credit Where It Is Due, Questions Where They Are Needed

In July, Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced that VAT on household electricity would fall from 5% to 0% for six months from 1 October, until the end of the financial year in March. It is expected to take about £45 off the annual cap and cost around £850 million in 2026/27. Ofgem itself said that without it, customers would have faced even higher costs this winter. I will give them that.

But look at how it was paid for. The government pointed to cancelling the Digital ID programme, which was expected to cost £600 million, leaving a £250 million shortfall by ITV's reckoning. Kemi Badenoch said: "You can't use the funding from an unfunded programme to pay for cheaper bills." Let's be honest: when someone cancels something that was never funded and calls it a saving, families should ask who picks up the tab later.

Consumer expert Martin Lewis said "in practice people won't feel much benefit". When the money-saving expert is telling you that, believe him.

It Is Not Just a Winter Problem

Ofgem says the rise is driven by high international gas prices linked to the ongoing Middle East conflict, with wholesale prices up 11% over three months. It adds that the cap is 7% higher than at the same time last year after adjusting for inflation. Britain has gas and wind and a North Sea we are choosing to run down. We are exposed to every shock abroad because of choices made here.

Where Reform UK Stands

Energy bills should be set by British resources and British common sense, not by wars thousands of miles away. Reform UK would back North Sea oil and gas rather than strangling it, scrap the green levies that sit on every bill, and stop treating cheap, reliable power as something to be rationed. A six-month VAT trim is a plaster. Families need a plan that brings bills down for good.
