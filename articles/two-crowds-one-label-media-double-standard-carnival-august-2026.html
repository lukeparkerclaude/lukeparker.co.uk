Stabbings, Sex Offences and 240 Arrests — but Only One London Crowd Gets Called 'Far-Right'

Forget the crowd sizes for a moment and look only at what was done. One London event this summer saw stabbings, sexual offences and 55 arrests for assaulting police in a single day. It was reported as 'overwhelmingly positive'. A political march with a fraction of the offending was branded 'far-right'. That is the double standard — and it is measured in crimes, not headcounts.

Let's not argue about how many people turned up where. Let's do something simpler and fairer: judge each crowd by the offences committed. That is the honest measure — and it is exactly the measure the media refuses to apply evenly.

The Carnival: What Was Actually Done

At this year's Notting Hill Carnival, guarded by more than 7,000 officers, the Metropolitan Police made over 240 arrests on the first day alone. The offences included possession of weapons, possession of drugs, assaults and sexual offences. There were multiple knife attacks. And 55 of those arrests were for assaulting police officers — in a single day. Officers leaned on live facial recognition, which the year before had flagged dozens of wanted people and registered sex offenders in the crowd.

Stabbings. Sexual offences. Dozens of officers attacked. And the headline that greeted it all? "Mood overwhelmingly positive." A triumph. A celebration. The knife crime and the sexual offences reduced to a footnote — if they were mentioned at all.

The March: What Was Actually Done

Now take the "Unite the Kingdom" march last September — a large, peaceful political demonstration. The offending there was, by comparison, tiny: around 25 arrests. Some officers were injured, and that was rightly reported. But the offences were a small fraction of what unfolded at the Carnival.

And the framing? Total, and singular: "far-right." Every bulletin. Every front page. The entire crowd — overwhelmingly law-abiding people — defined wall-to-wall by its handful of bad actors and branded extremist.

Count the Crimes, Not the Excuses

So line them up honestly. Far more arrests, actual stabbings, sexual offences and 55 assaults on police at one event — reported as a joyous success. Far fewer offences at the other — reported as a threat to the nation. If offending is the yardstick, the coverage is exactly upside down.

And to be fair, because this only works if it is honest: the vast majority at both events committed no crime at all. Most Carnival-goers are there for a genuine cultural celebration. Most marchers were there to wave a flag and go home. That is precisely the point. Apply one standard. If you judge a crowd by its criminal minority, judge both that way. If you extend the benefit of the doubt to a peaceful majority, extend it to both.

Follow the Language

Watch which crowd gets the collective label. Wrongdoing at the Carnival becomes "a number of isolated incidents." Wrongdoing at a patriotic march becomes "the far-right." One crowd's offenders are treated as individuals; the other crowd's offenders are treated as the crowd itself. That is not neutral reporting. It is a political choice about who gets the benefit of the doubt — and who is guilty by association.

And Follow the Resources

Seven thousand officers for the Carnival. Thousands more, and a multi-million-pound bill, for policing the marches. This is public money and public manpower — and the public is owed an honest account of the offences at every event, not a running order sorted by which crowd the newsroom happens to approve of.

Where Reform UK Stands

One standard. For everyone. Report the offences honestly wherever they happen — a stabbing is a stabbing and an assault on a police officer is a crime, whether the flag overhead is a national one or a cultural one. Back our police at every event, without exception. And drop the selective "far-right" branding that damns a peaceful crowd for the actions of a few while waving through far more serious offending somewhere more fashionable. The British public can see the double standard perfectly well. They are simply waiting for someone to say it out loud.

Source: Notting Hill Carnival 2026 (60th): 7,000+ officers deployed (largest recent operation); more than 240 arrests on the first day for offences including possession of weapons, possession of drugs, assaults and sexual offences; 55 of those arrests for assaults on police officers; multiple knife/slashing incidents; live facial recognition used — Metropolitan Police, ITV News London and GB News, 30 Aug 2026. 'Unite the Kingdom' march, London, 13 September 2025: a large political demonstration with around 25 arrests for offences including violent disorder and assaults, and some officers injured — CBS News, PBS and Euronews, September 2025.
