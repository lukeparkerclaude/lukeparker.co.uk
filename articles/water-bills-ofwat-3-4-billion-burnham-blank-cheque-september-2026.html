Water Bills: £3.4bn More Approved While Burnham Says Customers Aren't a 'Blank Cheque'

The regulator has provisionally approved an extra £3.4 billion of spending for five water companies, on top of bill rises already agreed. The Prime Minister says households are not a ‘blank cheque’. The consultation has just closed.

You couldn't make it up. Water customers have been told to expect yet more spending on their bills — while the Prime Minister says households “should not be treated as a bottomless source of funding.”

The Numbers

Five water suppliers, including Thames Water, have been provisionally approved by Ofwat for an extra £3.4 billion in spending that can be recovered through bills between 2027 and 2030. That comes on top of overall bill increases of 36% already approved for 2025–2030, and an average 5.4% increase that came in from April 2026.

The Ofwat consultation ran until 24 September, with a final decision due in December 2026. Thames Water alone serves around 16 million customers and carries more than £20 billion of debt.

Talk Versus Action

Andy Burnham said customers “cannot be treated as a blank cheque”. Environment Secretary Angela Eagle called current regulation “toothless” and promised to “fundamentally reform” the sector. The Consumer Council for Water says Ofwat must show every pound of the £3.4 billion is necessary and delivers value.

Here's my honest read: strong words are easy. The test is what the December decision says. Households have paid more and more while sewage spills and supply failures have carried on. If the regulator is toothless, that is a government problem to fix, not a customer problem to fund.

Where Reform UK Stands

Reform UK's position is simple: customers should not keep paying more for failure. Water companies must be held to account — with bills, bonuses and dividends tied to actual performance — and a regulator that acts on behalf of the public, not the balance sheet.
