9 Crimes Per Thief: The Shoplifting Epidemic Exposing Labour's Soft Justice

The Centre for Social Justice has published the numbers. Convicted shoplifters now commit an average of 9.1 offences each — up from 5.5 five years ago. Two-thirds reoffend within twelve months. Labour's response has been a 10-year plan and a press release.

The numbers from the Centre for Social Justice are extraordinary. The average shoplifter, by the time they are convicted, has committed 9.1 offences. Five years ago that figure was 5.5. Two-thirds of convicted shoplifters reoffend within a year of leaving court. The pre-pandemic figure was 55%; it is now 67%. That is not a statistical wobble. That is a system that has stopped working.

Police-recorded shop theft hit 516,971 offences in the year to December 2024 — a 20% year-on-year jump and the highest figure since the current Home Office counting rules came in. The 2025 figure dropped slightly to 509,566, but only because retailers have given up reporting incidents they know will not be investigated. Independent retail surveys put the true number at over a million. British high streets are quietly being looted.

The Salford Mass-Shoplifting Case

The end of last month gave us the case that brought it all home. Greater Manchester Police charged 15 people in connection with what was described as an organised shoplifting plot operating across Salford and surrounding areas — coordinated raids on supermarkets, baby formula and high-value cosmetics being the main targets, the goods then resold online and at car boot sales. This is not opportunistic theft by desperate people. This is organised crime treating retail premises as warehouses to be raided at will.

And the response from law enforcement has been valiant where it has happened — credit to the officers involved — but pitifully under-resourced. The Met has openly admitted it does not investigate shop thefts under £200. Forces around the country have variations on the same theme. The criminals know this. They calibrate their thefts accordingly. The result is the 9.1 offences per convicted thief: it now takes nearly twice as many crimes before the system catches up.

The Sentencing Act Made It Worse

What Labour has done since coming into office has actively made things worse. The Sentencing Act 2026 — the early-release legislation rushed through to deal with the prisons crisis — has accelerated the conveyor belt out of the prison gates. Persistent shoplifters who would once have served real time now serve a fraction. The Crime and Policing Bill, currently going through Parliament, finally creates a specific offence of assaulting a retail worker — something Reform and the Conservatives both supported — but it does nothing about the volume problem.

The volume problem is the real issue. If a thief can commit nine offences before being caught and most are out within months of being sentenced, the system is not deterring crime — it is processing it. Shopkeepers know this. They tell me. Every time I visit a high street in the constituency the conversation is the same: cameras everywhere, security tags on everything, doors locked at half five because the late shift cannot be staffed safely. This is not 1990s Britain. This is now.

Labour's 10-Year Plan

The Prime Minister announced a 'plan to halve knife crime within a decade' a few weeks ago. Set aside for a moment that violent street crime should not require a decade — set aside that any government promising results in 2035 is by definition not promising them on its own watch — and consider what that decade-long timescale tells you about the seriousness of the response. A shopkeeper losing £200 of stock today does not have ten years. A pensioner who has been mugged for her shopping bag does not have ten years. The retail worker assaulted on shift last week does not have ten years.

What Britain needs is not a ten-year plan. It is enforcement, this year. It is officers on the streets, this year. It is courts that sit through the backlog, this year. It is a Crown Prosecution Service that prosecutes shop theft as a serious volume crime and a Sentencing Council that backs them. None of that is in any current government policy paper.

What Reform UK Would Do

Reform UK has set out a clear plan. We would scrap the £200 threshold for police investigation of shop theft outright — every reported theft investigated, every time. We would back zero tolerance policing, properly resourced, with the volume of officers and the legal backing to do the job. We would reverse the Sentencing Act early-release provisions for prolific offenders. We would expand prison capacity rather than emptying prisons to manage capacity. And we would recognise that organised retail crime is exactly that — organised crime — and apply the same techniques used against drug networks: surveillance, asset seizure, and conspiracy charges where evidence supports them.

The Centre for Social Justice has done the country a service by publishing these figures. They confirm what every shop owner, every retail worker and every honest customer in this country already knows. Britain's high streets have been abandoned by a justice system that has lost the will to enforce the law. Reform UK is the only party serious about putting that right. The 9.1 offences per thief is a national disgrace. It is also a national choice, and it can be reversed.

Source: Centre for Social Justice analysis of shoplifting reoffending and offence frequency, April 2026; Office for National Statistics Crime Survey for England and Wales, year ending December 2025; House of Commons Library briefing on the Crime and Policing Bill 2024-26.
