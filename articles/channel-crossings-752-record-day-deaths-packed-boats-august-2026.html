752 in a Day: The Channel Crossing Record That Exposes Burnham\u2019s Empty \u2018Relentless\u2019 Pledge

On 29 July, 752 migrants crossed the Channel in a single day \u2014 the highest daily figure of 2026 \u2014 while four people died in the crossings. Days later, Prime Minister Andy Burnham promised to be \u201crelentless\u201d on small boats. The numbers tell a different story.

On 29 July 2026, 752 people crossed the English Channel in small boats in a single day — the highest daily figure recorded anywhere in 2026. Nine boats made the crossing. Four migrants died in the attempt, three women found unresponsive off Dunkirk the following morning. Days later, Prime Minister Andy Burnham stood up and promised his government would be “relentless” in tackling small boat crossings. The numbers from his own first weeks in office suggest otherwise.

A Record Broken, Four Lives Lost

The scale of the 29 July crossing should have been a moment of reckoning. Nearly 100 people were crammed onto each boat on average that day — a packing ratio that has made these crossings more dangerous than ever, even as overall numbers have fallen this year. Four people did not survive the journey. This is what “controlled migration” looks like under a government that talks tough and delivers little.

Every one of these deaths was preventable. They are the direct consequence of a policy environment that has allowed criminal smuggling gangs to operate with near impunity across northern France, packing more people onto fewer boats to maximise profit while minimising the number of crossings that get intercepted.

The “Relentless” Promise, Undercut by the Numbers

Andy Burnham took over as Prime Minister on 20 July 2026, and one of his first acts on the world stage was to vow to be “relentless” in stopping the boats. Within days, Britain had recorded its highest daily arrival figure of the year. That is not relentlessness. That is a government reacting to a crisis it has no real plan to solve.

Reform UK has said for years that warm words from Labour ministers — and now from a Labour Prime Minister who inherited the same failed Home Office machinery — count for nothing without enforcement, deterrence, and a returns agreement with real teeth. Nothing about the record-breaking crossing in July suggests any of that is in place.

Smaller Fall, Fuller Boats

Ministers will point to the fact that total crossings this year are running below last year’s pace: some 14,526 people had arrived by 1 August 2026, down 43% on the 25,436 recorded at the same point in 2025. That is true, and it is worth acknowledging. But it is only half the picture. The average number of people per boat is running at a record high — meaning smuggling gangs are adapting, not disappearing, cramming more people into each vessel to keep their operation profitable even as enforcement makes individual crossings harder to arrange.

A falling headline number achieved by packing more desperate people onto each dangerously overloaded boat is not a policy success. It is a warning sign that the underlying trade in human lives across the Channel is as active as ever — and getting more dangerous per crossing, not less.

What Reform UK Would Do

Reform UK would end the incentives that make the Channel route worth the risk in the first place: a genuine removals and returns policy, offshore processing of claims, and a clear message that arriving illegally does not lead to settlement in Britain. Downing Street can promise to be “relentless” all it likes. Until the crossings actually stop, and until lives stop being lost in the Channel on Burnham’s watch, that word means nothing.
