Frozen Until 2031: Healey's Stealth Tax on Every Working Family

On Wednesday Chancellor John Healey went before the Treasury Committee and defended his decision to keep income tax thresholds frozen until April 2031. It is the tax rise that never has to be announced, and it lands on exactly the people who can least afford it.

Labour promised not to raise income tax on working people. Then the Budget on 28 October froze income tax thresholds until April 2031. On Wednesday, Chancellor John Healey sat in front of the Treasury Committee and defended it.

The Tax Rise Nobody Has to Announce

Here is how a threshold freeze works. Your rate of tax stays the same on paper. But as your wages go up, even just to keep pace with prices, more of your pay crosses into tax, and more people get dragged into the higher-rate band. The government collects more without ever having to stand up and say "we are putting up income tax."

That is why it gets called a stealth tax. And a freeze stretching to 2031 means years more of it, for nurses, care workers, electricians and shop managers, not just the very wealthy.

And That's Not All in the Budget

The same Budget raises income tax rates on property and savings income by two percentage points from 6 April 2027, to 24% basic, 44% higher and 49% additional rate. Anyone with a buy-to-let flat or a bit put away in savings will feel it.

Meanwhile the government's own room for manoeuvre is shrinking. Fiscal headroom falls from £23.7bn to £15bn, and borrowing costs are up £14bn this financial year. Growth is forecast at just 1.2% for 2026/27 and 1% for 2027/28. More tax, less growth, less headroom. That is not a plan, it's a squeeze.

A Budget That Leaked

Part of the same committee session was spent on how details of a bank tax change reached Bloomberg before the Budget. The Treasury's permanent secretary, James Bowler, called it "deeply regrettable and frustrating" and admitted "markets moved a little bit on the release of this information." An inquiry is under way. If you are going to tax people this hard, the least you can do is run a tight ship.

Where Reform UK Stands

Let's be honest: freezing thresholds is a tax rise by another name. Reform UK wants to take low and middle earners out of tax by lifting thresholds, not freezing them, and to pay for it by getting a grip on waste in Whitehall rather than squeezing working families year after year. People in Lancashire are not asking for much. They are asking to keep more of what they earn.
