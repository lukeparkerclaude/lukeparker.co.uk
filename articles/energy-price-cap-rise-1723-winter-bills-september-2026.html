Your Energy Bill Is Going Up Again — and Britain Is Still Importing the Problem

From tomorrow the typical household's energy cap goes from £1,663 to £1,723. Ofgem says international gas prices are to blame. Let's be honest about the bigger question: why is a country sitting on North Sea gas so exposed to them?

From tomorrow, 1 October, the typical household's energy bill goes up again. Ofgem has confirmed the price cap rises 4%, taking the typical dual-fuel bill on direct debit from £1,663 to £1,723 a year — an extra £60, or about £5 a month. It applies from 1 October to 31 December.

Sixty quid will not sound like much to Westminster. To a family already juggling the mortgage, the shopping and the school run, it is one more bill that only ever goes one way.

What Ofgem Actually Says

Ofgem's own explanation is that high international gas prices are continuing to drive energy costs in the UK. Gas bills rise by around 8% this quarter. The regulator's Director General for Markets, Neil Kenward, put it plainly: “High international gas prices are continuing to drive energy costs in the UK.” The unit rates for the quarter are 7.97p per kWh for gas and 26.32p per kWh for electricity.

Fair is fair: the Government has removed VAT from household electricity from 1 October until 31 March 2027, and Ofgem itself says that without it customers would have faced even higher costs this winter. That is a real help, and I will say so. Ofgem also notes the cap remains 52% below the 2022 crisis peak of £2,500, and that around 35% of households are on fixed tariffs and are not affected by this rise.

The Question Nobody in Westminster Wants to Answer

Here is my honest read. A VAT cut on electricity is a plaster. The wound is that our bills are set by what happens in international gas markets, while we tie our own hands on producing more of our own. Every time there is a shock abroad, British households pay for it at home — and every time, the answer from Westminster is a temporary tax tweak rather than a plan to be less exposed.

That is an opinion, and I hold it firmly: a serious country with gas under its own seabed does not shrug and accept that its families will be at the mercy of the world price every winter.

Where Reform UK Stands

Reform UK’s position is simple: bring bills down by producing more of our own energy and by cutting the green levies and policy costs loaded onto them. Back North Sea gas rather than importing it, stop treating households as the piggy bank for net zero, and give families a plan rather than a plaster. Temporary VAT holidays are welcome — but they expire. Cheap, secure energy is a policy for the long term.
