Crossings Are Down. So Why Did Two Boats Land Without Anyone Noticing?

Small boat crossings are lower than last year, and I will say so. But two boats landed undetected within two days, and the Home Secretary herself called it 'not acceptable'.

I want this argument to be bulletproof, so let me start with the number the government will quote at you. It is true. Provisional Home Office figures reported by ITV News on 24 September showed 18,565 people had crossed the Channel in small boats so far in 2026, against 32,188 at the same point in 2025. That is a fall of 42%. Credit where it is due.

The Part They Don't Put in the Press Release

Here is the same report. The busiest day of 2026 so far came on Wednesday 23 September, with 781 arrivals. And Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood ordered an urgent review after two boats landed undetected within two days. She called the failure to intercept them "not acceptable" and said "even one" uncontrolled landing is "one too many".

I agree with her. And let's be honest about what that means: a country that does not know who has landed on its shores is not a country in control of its border. A lower number is welcome. It does not answer that.

The Numbers Keep Moving

The government has been keen to claim credit. In early August Burnham said crossings were down 50% on last year; the Home Office figures ITV cited at the time showed a fall of 43%. Over the same report, the 2,057 people who crossed after he became Prime Minister on 20 July were more than the 1,962 over the same period in 2025.

The autumn has not been quiet either. Between 1 and 7 October, 1,739 people crossed, including 609 on the Monday alone, in eight vessels, an average of about 76 people per boat, according to figures reported by the Press Association. Two men died in the Channel that Monday morning. The year-to-date total in that report stood at 20,935.

What Needs to Happen

Burnham has blamed Brexit for weakening cooperation with neighbours. My honest read is that excuses will not stop a single dinghy. The people smugglers are running a business, and the way to end it is to make sure that arriving illegally never leads to staying here.

Where Reform UK Stands

Control means knowing who comes in, and being able to remove those who have no right to be here. Reform UK will back every measure that genuinely stops the boats and will say so when something works. But a fall in the numbers is not the same as a secure border, and a landing nobody spotted is a failure of the one job the state cannot afford to get wrong.

Source: ITV News, 24 September 2026 (18,565 vs 32,188, down 42%; 781 arrivals on 23 September; Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood's review of two undetected landings and quotes); ITV News, 2 August 2026 (Burnham's 50% claim vs 43% in Home Office figures; 2,057 crossings since 20 July vs 1,962 in the same period of 2025); Press Association via GB News, 8 October 2026 (1-7 October: 1,739 crossings, 609 on Monday in eight vessels, 20,935 year to date, two deaths, Burnham's Brexit comment).
