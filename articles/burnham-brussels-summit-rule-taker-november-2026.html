Burnham Goes to Brussels. Britain Risks Coming Home a Rule-Taker.

Today the Prime Minister sits down with the EU's top brass in Brussels. On the table: following EU food rules, a reported 100,000 work visas a year for young Europeans, and a 'national conversation' that could include rejoining. Nobody voted for this.

Today, Friday 20 November, Andy Burnham sits down in Brussels with European Council President António Costa and Commission President Ursula von der Leyen. It is only the second UK-EU summit since we left. Downing Street says the talks are "going down to the wire". At the time of writing we don't know the final outcome, so I'll stick to what has been reported going in. It is worrying enough.

What's on the Table

According to ITV News and the Associated Press, the expected deals cover food and drink trade, emissions trading and youth mobility. The food deal would see Britain align with EU rules on "sanitary and phytosanitary" goods such as food and plants. ITV reports the sticking point is how much say the UK would have over future EU food rules.

On youth mobility, ITV reports the UK agreed to an EU demand for 100,000 work visas a year for young Europeans, with the age limit raised from 30 to 31, after British negotiators had initially pushed for a lower cap. If agreed, Burnham's press secretary says legislation will come to Parliament next week.

The Prime Minister's own pitch is that the deals could add "a modest 0.4%" to the economy. This week he also said: "The Brexit we were given has not worked. We have lost more than we gained."

No Say, No Vote, No Veto

Here's the heart of it. If you sign up to follow another body's rules but don't get a vote on what those rules are, you are a rule-taker. The Conservatives' Victoria Atkins put it well: farmers would be "forced to follow rules over which we have no say, no vote, and no veto." Nigel Farage warned on GB News that "the British Parliament will have no say. Any dispute will be judged by the European Court."

Let's be honest: that is not taking back control. It is handing it back quietly, one sector at a time.

"Not About Rejoining" — Until It Is

Burnham says 73% of Britons support closer ties and insists: "That's not about rerunning 2016. It's not about rejoining." Yet ITV reports he is preparing a "national conversation" on the future relationship which could potentially include seeking to regain full membership. Both of those things cannot be the whole story.

Farage called the deal "a total betrayal of what Andy Burnham promised the people of Makerfield just a few months ago." My honest read: people in towns like ours voted to leave, were told it would be respected, and are now watching it be unpicked without a fresh mandate.

Where Reform UK Stands

Britain should trade with Europe as a friend, not follow its rules as a junior partner. Any deal that binds us to laws we can't vote on, or adds 100,000 work visas a year while legal migration is still far too high, should be put plainly to Parliament and to the public — not rushed through the week after a summit. And if the Prime Minister wants to reopen the question of EU membership, he should say so openly and seek a mandate for it at a general election.
