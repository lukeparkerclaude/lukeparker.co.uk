Energy Bills Up Again: Ofgem Cap Hits £1,723 From October

Ofgem has confirmed the price cap rises by 4% to £1,723 a year from 1 October, up £60, with gas bills climbing 8%. Even after the government's VAT cut on electricity, the direction of travel is the same one families have lived with for years: up.

Here we go again. On 26 August, Ofgem confirmed that the energy price cap will rise by 4% from 1 October, taking the typical annual bill for a household using gas and electricity from £1,663 to £1,723. That's another £60 a year, or £5 a month, landing just as the heating goes back on across Lancashire.

Gas Is Doing the Damage

Dig into Ofgem's own figures and the picture is clear. Gas bills are rising by 8%. Electricity is broadly flat, but only because the government has removed VAT from domestic electricity bills. Ofgem says that without the VAT cut the rise would have been around £45 higher. So the government's intervention hasn't brought bills down. It has softened yet another increase. Ofgem also notes that, adjusted for inflation, the new cap is 7% higher than the same period last year.

Around 22 million households on default tariffs are covered by the cap. Roughly 11 million on fixed deals won't see this particular rise, but they're already paying whatever price they locked in during the last round of increases.

Why Are We Still Here?

Ofgem blames higher wholesale gas prices driven by conflict in the Middle East, and it's true that global markets are volatile. But let's be honest: Britain sits on its own gas in the North Sea and under the ground in Lancashire, and successive governments have chosen to leave it there while importing energy at whatever price the world market sets. My honest read is that a country that refuses to use its own resources has no right to act surprised when it ends up at the mercy of everyone else's.

Every rise like this hits the people with the least room to absorb it: pensioners on fixed incomes, families already juggling the mortgage and the food shop, small businesses in our towns. A £5-a-month headline figure sounds small in Whitehall. It doesn't feel small in Chorley or Burnley in January.

Where Reform UK Stands

Reform UK's position is straightforward. Scrap the net zero levies loaded onto bills, fast-track North Sea oil and gas licences, and lift the ban on shale gas so Britain produces more of its own energy instead of importing it. Cheaper, home-grown energy is the only long-term route to lower bills. Cutting VAT is a sticking plaster; the government is treating the symptom while refusing to touch the cause. Until that changes, expect to see this headline again in three months' time.

Sources: Ofgem press release, "Energy price cap will rise by 4% from October 2026" (26 August 2026); MoneySavingExpert, "Energy Price Cap still to rise from 1 October even after the Government's VAT cut" (August 2026).
