April 2027: The State Pension Finally Crosses the Tax Line

For years the warning was theoretical: freeze the tax-free allowance for long enough and the state pension will eventually poke above it. With the earnings figure for the triple lock now in at 3.9%, that stops being a warning and becomes a date. From April 2027, on the numbers as they stand, a full new state pension on its own is above the personal allowance — and the Treasury has said nothing about it.

Every so often a piece of arithmetic that has been creeping up on the country for years finally arrives, and everybody in Westminster suddenly finds something else to talk about. This is one of those weeks.

The earnings figure that feeds the triple lock is in. Average weekly earnings including bonuses, for the three months to July 2026, came in at 3.9% — currently the highest of the three measures the triple lock takes, and therefore the one that is likely to set next April's rise.

On that basis the full new state pension goes from £241.30 a week to £250.70 a week from April 2027. An extra £9.40 a week, reported as around £488 a year.

Now do the annual sum. £250.70 a week is £13,036.40 a year.

And the personal allowance — the amount you may earn before income tax starts — is frozen at £12,570.

The Line Has Been Crossed

This year, 2026/27, the full new state pension is £12,547.60. That is £22.40 below the allowance. Twenty-two pounds and forty pence of headroom. That is how close it has been.

From April 2027, on the figures as they currently stand, the full new state pension is £466.40 above the allowance. At the basic rate of 20%, that is a little over £93 of income tax arising on a state pension — for people whose only income is the state pension the government itself sets.

Let's be honest about what has happened here. Nobody voted for this. No minister stood up and announced a new tax on pensioners. It was done by freezing a threshold and then waiting. That is the entire mechanism. Fiscal drag does the work, the politician keeps their hands clean, and the pensioner opens a letter they do not understand.

One caveat, because accuracy matters more than a good line: the final uprating is not formally confirmed until September's inflation figure is published on 20 October, since the triple lock takes the highest of earnings, inflation or 2.5%. Inflation is not expected to come in above 3.9%. But if it does, the pension rises by more — and crosses the line by more.

The Bit That Should Make You Angry

The state pension is not a benefit. It is a contributory entitlement, paid for by a lifetime of National Insurance on money that was already taxed once when it was earned.

So the arrangement we are now arriving at is this: you pay tax on your wages, you pay National Insurance on your wages, you reach pension age, the state hands back a sum it decided, and then it taxes that too.

And the political class have known this crossover was coming for years. The allowance freeze has been in place and extended, and every serious forecaster could see exactly where the two lines met. It was allowed to happen because it raises money quietly, and because pensioners are assumed not to make a fuss.

My honest read: the Treasury is hoping you do not notice until the 2027/28 tax year is halfway through and it is too late to be a story.

What Happens Next

The Chancellor, John Healey, delivers his first Budget on 28 October. He has a straightforward choice in front of him.

He can lift the personal allowance so that the state pension sits safely beneath it. He can introduce a specific exemption so that the state pension is not taxed. Or he can say nothing at all, bank the revenue, and let the letters land in the spring.

Watch which one he picks. It will tell you everything about what this government thinks pensioners are for.

Where Reform UK Stands

Nobody should pay income tax on the state pension. Full stop.

Reform UK's position is that the tax-free allowance has to rise, and rise substantially — that is the honest answer to fiscal drag, rather than tinkering at the edges while the freeze quietly does the collecting. And it is unambiguous that the triple lock must be protected, not because it is politically convenient but because a contributory pension that shrinks in real terms is a promise broken.

The freeze on thresholds is the biggest tax rise nobody ever announced. It has been dragging working people into higher bands for years, and in April 2027 it reaches the pensioners as well. Either the allowance goes up, or the government should have the courage to stand at the despatch box and say out loud that it is now taxing the state pension. What it should not do is let a spreadsheet do the deed and hope nobody checks the maths.
