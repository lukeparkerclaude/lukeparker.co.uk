Healey Hits Savers and Landlords With a 2p Tax Rise – and the OBR Says Rents Will Pay the Price

Tax on savings and rental income goes up by two points from April 2027, the second rise in as many years. Even the OBR expects renters to feel it.

Labour told us it was targeting the wealthy. Yesterday’s Budget targeted savers and landlords, and the people who rent from them are going to feel it too.

What the Budget Did

According to Tax Policy Associates, tax rates on savings and property income rise by two percentage points from 6 April 2027, to 24%, 44% and 49%. That follows a two-point rise last year. The measure is permanent and raises roughly £1bn. Landlords’ finance cost relief is also raised to the 24% basic rate.

The Guardian reported that the property and savings tax rise was unexpected.

The Watchdog Warns Renters

This is the part Labour would rather you skipped. The Office for Budget Responsibility thinks the change is “more likely than not” to push up rents in the long run, according to Tax Policy Associates. The Guardian quotes the OBR as warning of the risk of “a steady rise in rents.”

Let’s be honest about who that hurts. It is the young couple trying to save a deposit, the family renting in a Lancashire town, the pensioner living on interest from a modest pot. Landlords can pass costs on. Tenants cannot pass them on to anyone.

Where Reform UK Stands

Reform UK would not punish people for saving or for providing homes. We want more homes built, a planning system that works, and a tax system that rewards effort rather than penalising it. You do not fix a housing crisis by taxing the people who provide housing and then acting surprised when rents rise.
