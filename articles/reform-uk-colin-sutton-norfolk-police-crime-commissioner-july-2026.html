Reform UK Wins Norfolk Police and Crime Commissioner as Voters Reject the Uniparty

Colin Sutton, a former Metropolitan Police Detective Chief Inspector, has been elected Norfolk's Police and Crime Commissioner for Reform UK — taking more votes than the Conservatives and Labour combined in what was once true-blue Tory territory.

Norfolk has spoken, and it has rejected the old parties in emphatic fashion. Colin Sutton, Reform UK's candidate for Police and Crime Commissioner, has won the seat with 32,647 votes — more than 14,000 ahead of the Conservatives on 18,343. Labour, which had previously held the PCC role, collapsed to fifth place with just 14,192 votes. Reform UK's total was greater than the Conservative and Labour vote combined. That is not a protest vote. That is a realignment.

A Former Tory Stronghold Falls

This result matters because of where it happened. The Conservatives took 45% of first-preference votes in Norfolk's 2021 PCC election. That share fell to 33.9% in 2024, and has now collapsed to just 15% — a drop of almost 19 percentage points and a loss of more than half their remaining vote share in this by-election alone. Tory leader Kemi Badenoch has spent recent weeks talking up a supposed "Kemi bounce." Norfolk's voters have delivered the opposite verdict. There is no bounce. There is a continued collapse of a party that no longer represents the people who used to vote for it.

Labour's performance was arguably worse. A governing party that installed Andy Burnham as Prime Minister without a general election has now been pushed into fifth place in a police and crime commissioner race in a county it once expected to win comfortably. Voters are not rewarding either of the old parties for their record in office. They are looking elsewhere.

The Right Man for the Job

Unlike career politicians who take on policing brief without ever having worked a beat, Colin Sutton brings three decades of frontline policing experience to the role. He began his career in uniform before rising to Detective Chief Inspector and Senior Investigating Officer at the Metropolitan Police, where he led more than 30 successful murder investigations, including the cases that caught serial killer Levi Bellfield and the "Night Stalker" rapist Delroy Grant. He has lived in and around Norfolk for the past 14 years.

Sutton has set out a clear priority: restoring "commonsense policing." That means more officers on foot patrol, wider use of stop-and-search, and reopening police counters to the public so residents can actually speak to an officer face to face. He has committed to focusing resources on the crimes that blight ordinary people's lives — anti-social behaviour, fly-tipping and rural equipment theft — rather than, in his words, officers spending their time policing social media posts. Reform UK has also pledged to scrap DEI policies within the force, end two-tier policing, and cut administrative waste to deliver better value for Norfolk taxpayers.

"It's an honour that so many people across our great county have voted for me to be their PCC," Sutton said following his win. "Having led major investigations in law enforcement for many years, I know what's needed to hit the ground running." He added that Norfolk Constabulary had too often overlooked rural areas in favour of the county's cities and towns, and pledged that would change.

Part of a Bigger Pattern

This victory does not stand alone. It follows Reform UK's breakthrough in Norfolk's local elections in May, when the party surged from just two county council seats to 40 and took outright control of the authority. The Conservatives, by contrast, collapsed from 58 seats to eight. Nigel Farage said Sutton's decades of frontline experience meant he could "hit the ground running from day one," in pointed contrast to career politicians who take on policing roles having never worked in law enforcement themselves.

The pattern across the country is now unmistakable. Voters who have spent years choosing between two parties that delivered the same outcomes — rising crime, rising immigration, rising taxes — are choosing something different. Norfolk was a Tory county for a generation. Today, it has a Reform UK Police and Crime Commissioner with a policing background most politicians could only dream of. That is what happens when the uniparty stops listening.

Source: Reform UK, "Reform UK's Colin Sutton elected as Norfolk Police and Crime Commissioner," 17 July 2026.
