781 in One Day: Burnham Says the Border Plan Is 'Working'. Is It?

Let's be fair and start with the numbers the Government likes: crossings are down on last year. But 781 people in a single day, 230 in a single boat and a Home Secretary admitting failures are 'not acceptable' is not what a controlled border looks like.

Let me start by being fair, because I want this argument to be unanswerable. Small boat crossings this year are lower than last year. The provisional figure reported by ITV News on 24 September was 18,565, which is 42% below the 32,188 at the same point in 2025. If you want a headline saying the numbers are falling, that is the number. It is real, and I will not pretend otherwise.

But a falling total is not the same thing as a border under control. Look at what else has happened.

781 in a Single Day

On Wednesday 23 September, 781 people were recorded arriving in small boats, the busiest single day of 2026 so far. ITV reported an average of around 78 people per boat that week. Even the Home Secretary, Shabana Mahmood, called the failure to intercept two boats "not acceptable". A Government spokesperson admitted: "we need to push further and harder".

Let's be honest: ministers do not use language like that about a plan that is solved.

230 People in One Boat

On 10 August, a record 230 people crossed in a single boat, beating the previous record of 165, set only that July. Earlier that month a dinghy carrying 173 people capsized after its engine caught fire. These are not neat statistics. They are overloaded boats on one of the busiest shipping lanes in the world, run by criminal gangs who care nothing for human life.

Nigel Farage put it simply: "This is a record number in a single vessel."

Whose Numbers?

On 2 August, Andy Burnham claimed crossings were down 50% this year. ITV pointed out that the provisional figures showed 43%, a different measure. ITV also reported that in the period since Burnham became Prime Minister on 20 July, 2,057 people crossed, against 1,962 in the same period of 2025, which is roughly 5% higher. Burnham himself conceded that "the public don't like the idea that the border looks like it's not controlled."

My honest read: the Prime Minister has just told you what the public think. The question is whether anyone in Westminster is prepared to do what it takes to change it.

Where Reform UK Stands

A controlled border means nobody gets to claim a win while hundreds still arrive in a day. Reform UK's position is that Britain must decide who comes here, that illegal arrivals should not be rewarded with a place in this country, and that the gangs only profit if the journey works. Fewer boats than last year is not the finish line. Control is.

All crossing figures are provisional Home Office figures as reported by ITV.
