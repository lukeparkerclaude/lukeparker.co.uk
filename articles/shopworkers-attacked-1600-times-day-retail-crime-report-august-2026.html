1,600 Attacks a Day: Britain's Shopworkers Are Paying the Price for Labour's Soft-Touch Justice

The British Retail Consortium's latest Crime Report puts a number on what shop staff have known for years: violence and abuse against retail workers now average 1,600 incidents a day — the second-highest total on record. Labour's new Crime and Policing Act finally scraps the £200 shoplifting loophole. It's a start. It isn't nearly enough.

Britain's shopworkers went to work this year facing violence and abuse on a scale that should shame any government. The British Retail Consortium's Crime Report 2026 puts a hard number on it: an average of 1,600 incidents of violence and abuse against shop staff every single day, including 118 physical assaults and 36 incidents involving a weapon — the second-highest total on record.

A Record Year for Retail Violence

Think about what that daily figure actually means. Somewhere in Britain, a shopworker is being physically assaulted roughly every twelve minutes of every working day. Someone is threatened with a weapon nearly forty times a day. These are ordinary people — checkout staff, shelf-stackers, security guards — doing an honest job, and paying for it with their safety because successive governments have allowed retail crime to spiral unchecked.

The £200 Loophole Is Gone — So What?

To its credit, the Crime and Policing Act 2026 finally scraps the rule that treated theft of goods worth £200 or less as a summary-only offence — a loophole that effectively told shoplifters the law wouldn't take small-scale theft seriously. Attacking a shopworker is now a standalone offence carrying up to six months in jail. These are overdue changes, not radical ones, and they arrive years after retailers and their staff started sounding the alarm.

The real test isn't what's written in the Act. It's whether police and courts actually use these new powers — consistently, and with the urgency that 1,600 incidents a day demands. Reform UK will be watching enforcement, not just legislation.

Recorded Crime Falls, Real Crime Doesn't

Police recorded 507,086 shoplifting offences in the year to March 2026, a 4% fall on the year before — a figure ministers will doubtless present as proof of progress. But retailers say that headline masks the true scale of the problem: 5.5 million detected shoplifting incidents in the same period, costing retailers nearly £400 million, with industry groups warning that huge numbers of incidents still go unreported because staff have lost confidence that reporting them will lead anywhere.

A falling official crime count alongside a rising, near-record daily toll of violence against shop staff tells you everything about the gap between statistics ministers like to quote and the reality on the shop floor.

What Reform UK Would Do

Reform UK would back a genuine zero-tolerance approach to retail crime — proper resourcing for neighbourhood policing so incidents are actually investigated, tougher minimum sentencing for repeat and violent offenders, and a justice system that treats an assault on a shopworker as seriously as an assault anywhere else. Britain's shop staff have carried the cost of soft-touch justice for too long. It's time enforcement caught up with the law.

Sources: British Retail Consortium, Crime Report 2026 (1,600 incidents a day; 118 physical violence, 36 involving a weapon); GOV.UK, Crime and Policing Act 2026 retail crime factsheet; ONS/police recorded crime statistics, shoplifting offences year to March 2026 (507,086, down 4%).
