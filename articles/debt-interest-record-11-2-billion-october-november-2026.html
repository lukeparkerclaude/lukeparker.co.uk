£11.2 Billion in One Month: The Interest Bill Nobody Voted For

In October alone, the government spent £11.2 billion just servicing the national debt. That is the highest October figure since monthly records began, and it is money that never builds a school, a road or a hospital ward.

Here is a number every taxpayer in Lancashire should know. In October 2026, central government spent £11.2 billion on debt interest. Not on nurses, not on police, not on potholes. On interest. That is according to the Office for National Statistics, which published the figures on Friday 20 November.

What the Figures Say

The ONS says October's debt interest bill was £2.8 billion (33.4%) higher than in October 2025, and the highest October figure since monthly records began in 1997. Of that, £4.2 billion was the inflation uplift on index-linked gilts, the government bonds that rise with RPI. So when inflation goes up, so does the cost of our debt.

Overall borrowing in October was £17.0 billion, £0.8 billion more than a year earlier and the third-highest October since records began in 1993. Public sector net debt now stands at £3,058.3 billion, equal to 95.8% of GDP. It passed £3 trillion for the first time in September.

The Fair Point, and the Real Point

I will be straight with you, because accuracy matters. Borrowing for the financial year so far, £111.3 billion, is £7.3 billion lower than the same period last year, as tax receipts have grown faster than spending. But it is still £3.3 billion above what the OBR forecast in March. And the Chief Secretary to the Treasury, Emma Reynolds, chose to describe all this as "standout resilience."

Let's be honest. Receipts growing faster than spending means you are paying more tax. And a debt interest bill that jumps by a third in a year is not resilience. It is a warning light.

Why It Matters to You

Every pound spent on interest is a pound that cannot go on frontline services or be left in your pocket. My honest read is that decades of governments of both colours have treated borrowing as free money, and now the bill is landing on working families through higher taxes and thinner services.

Where Reform UK Stands

Get a grip on the debt before it gets a grip on us. Reform UK wants real spending discipline: cut waste in Whitehall, stop the money pouring into failed schemes, and look hard at how the Bank of England's arrangements and index-linked borrowing are costing taxpayers. A government that spends £11.2 billion in a month on interest has no business telling you there is nothing left to cut.
