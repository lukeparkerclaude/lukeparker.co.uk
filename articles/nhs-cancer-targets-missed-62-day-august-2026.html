Every Cancer Target Missed: Just 69.9% Start Treatment Within 62 Days

NHS England missed all three cancer waiting time standards in June. Just 69.9% of patients began treatment within 62 days, against a target of 85% — a standard not met since December 2015.

There is a number in the latest NHS England performance data that should stop anyone in their tracks. Of the patients referred urgently with suspected cancer in June, just 69.9% began treatment within 62 days. The target is 85%. It has not been met since December 2015 — more than a decade ago, under three different Prime Ministers.

For roughly three in every ten of those patients, the clock kept running. In cancer care, the clock is not an administrative abstraction. It is the thing that determines what the treatment can still achieve.

All Three Standards Missed

The figures, published on 13 August and covering June 2026, show every cancer standard breached:

  • 62-day referral to treatment: 69.9% against an 85% target
  • 31-day decision-to-treat: 92.2% against a 96% target, down from 92.8% in May
  • 28-day Faster Diagnosis Standard: 79.0% against an 80% target

The 31-day standard has not been met since December 2020. The Faster Diagnosis Standard was the closest to being hit, and it was still missed. Three standards, three failures, in the one area of medicine where delay is measured in outcomes rather than inconvenience.

The Rest of the Picture Is More Complicated

I could stop there, and it would make a cleaner argument. But it would not be the truth, and readers of this site are entitled to the whole of it.

The overall waiting list is not soaring. It stood at around 7.27 million pathways in June, broadly flat on May's 7.28 million and down about 95,500 — some 1.3% — on June last year. It sits roughly 400,000 below the peak of about 7.7 million reached in September 2023. Anyone quoting 7.75 million as a current figure is quoting 2023.

Performance against the 18-week standard has improved too: 65.8% in June, up from 65.6% in May and up 4.3 percentage points on June last year. The median wait has fallen to 11.9 weeks from 13.4 a year ago. Waits over a year are down around 45% on the year. June saw the busiest June on record for completed pathways, with roughly 1.66 million treatments finished.

So the system is treating more people, faster, than it was twelve months ago. That is real, and the staff doing it deserve to have it said.

Where the Warning Lights Are

The problem is what has happened in the last three months. Waits over 52 weeks have risen from 94,404 in March to 105,711 in June — an increase of nearly 12%, ending ten consecutive months of falls. A trend that had been improving steadily has turned, and turned quickly.

Against the 92% constitutional standard for 18-week treatment, 65.8% is not a near miss. The government's own target is to reach 92% by March 2029, which is an admission that patients are being asked to wait another two and a half years for the standard they are already legally entitled to.

Meanwhile A&E performance slipped to 75.4% in July from 75.7% in June, against a 95% standard that has not been met since July 2015. July brought a record 2.49 million A&E attendances — the busiest month ever recorded.

What Reform UK Would Do

Reform UK's argument has never been that NHS staff are not working hard enough. The record June activity figures prove the opposite. The argument is that too much of the money never reaches the front line. We would cut the layers of management and quango overhead between the Treasury and the ward, and redirect that spending into diagnostic capacity — scanners, endoscopy lists, pathology — because the 62-day pathway is overwhelmingly bottlenecked at diagnosis, not at the point of treatment.

We would also end the reliance on agency staffing that drains budgets while doing nothing for continuity of care, and train and retain British clinicians rather than treating overseas recruitment as a permanent fix for a domestic workforce failure.

The waiting list is coming down. Cancer performance is not. When a target has been missed continuously since 2015, the honest conclusion is not that we need to try harder at the current model. It is that the model is not delivering for the people who can least afford to wait.

Cancer standards for June 2026: 62-day 69.9% (85% target), 31-day 92.2% (96% target), 28-day Faster Diagnosis Standard 79.0% (80% target).
