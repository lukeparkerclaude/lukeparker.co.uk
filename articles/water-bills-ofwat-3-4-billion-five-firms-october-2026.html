Ofwat Waves Through £3.4 Billion More for Water Firms. Guess Who Pays.

Five water companies are provisionally cleared to put bills up between 2027 and 2030 to fund £3.4 billion of extra spending, while serious pollution incidents sit at record levels. The final decision lands in December.

Here we go again. Ofwat, the industry regulator, has given water companies provisional approval for £3.4 billion in extra spending. Five of the 13 firms, Thames Water, Severn Trent Water, Southern Water, Wessex Water and South East Water, are provisionally allowed to raise customer bills between 2027 and 2030 to pay for it.

The companies originally asked for £4.3 billion. So we are meant to be grateful that they only got most of it.

On Top of What You Already Pay

Remember what has already been signed off. Ofwat's earlier allowance was for bills to rise 36% between 2025 and 2030, plus an average 5.4% rise from April 2026.

Water UK says that "for most customers there will be no immediate impact on bills". Let's be honest about what "immediate" is doing in that sentence.

Even Burnham Is Saying It

Prime Minister Andy Burnham said: "Customers cannot be treated as a blank cheque." He added that customers have been asked to pay more for years, yet serious pollution incidents are at record levels. Environment Secretary Angela Eagle called regulation of the sector "toothless" and promised to "fundamentally reform" it.

I agree with the diagnosis. But notice: this is the Government criticising a regulator that is supposed to be on the side of the customer. Who has been in charge while it was toothless?

Thames Water

Thames Water has about 16 million customers and more than £20 billion of debt. It is one of the five firms provisionally allowed to raise bills. Ofwat says expenditure can be "clawed back" if companies fail to deliver. After years of watching delivery fail, forgive me if I want to see it before I believe it.

The review took three months. The consultation was due to close on 24 September, and the final decision is due in December, which means there is still time to get this right.

Where Reform UK Stands

No bill increase without delivery first. Families should not be asked to fund water companies' failures while pollution incidents are at record levels. Reform UK's position is that money must be tied to results, with real consequences for bosses and shareholders who fall short, and a regulator that actually acts for the customer.
