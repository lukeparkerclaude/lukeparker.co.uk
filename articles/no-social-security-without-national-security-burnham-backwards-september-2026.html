There Is No Social Security Without National Security. Burnham Has It Backwards.

At Prime Minister's Questions, Andy Burnham said national security 'can't come at the expense of social security'. It sounds compassionate. It is, in fact, the most dangerous inversion in politics — because there is no social security, no pension, no NHS and no welfare state at all in a country that cannot defend itself. He has the first duty of government exactly backwards.

At Prime Minister's Questions this week, Andy Burnham gave us a line that will be quoted back at him for a long time. Pressed on whether he would commit to spending 3% of our economy on defence by 2030, he said: "We do everything to support our national security, but it can't come at the expense of social security."

It is meant to sound compassionate. It is actually the most dangerous inversion in British politics — and it deserves to be taken apart.

The Order Is Not Optional

National security is not one competing priority among many. It is the foundation the others stand on. The pension, the NHS, universal credit, the entire welfare state — every last pound of social security exists because there is a secure, functioning country in which to provide it. Remove the security, and there is nothing left to distribute. You cannot pay a pension in a country that cannot defend its own borders. Ask Ukraine whether national security can wait behind social security.

So when the Prime Minister frames defence and welfare as a straight either/or — guns versus the vulnerable — he is not being kind. He is announcing that he does not understand the first duty of his own office. The defence of the realm and its citizens comes first, because everything else is built on top of it.

What This Line Is Really For

Let's be honest about why he reached for it. This was not a philosophy seminar — it was cover. The very same week, his Chancellor, John Healey — who resigned as Defence Secretary in June because the Treasury would not fund defence — confirmed he will shelve the 3%-by-2030 target in next month's Budget. "It can't come at the expense of social security" is the slogan that dresses up that climbdown as a moral choice rather than a broken promise.

And the timing could hardly be worse. Ministers themselves talk of "rising threats". Boats are landing on our own coastline, a few miles from the home of the Royal Navy. And the Government's message to the country is that defence is the thing we get to last — once everything else is paid for.

The False Choice

To be fair, no one sensible wants to see vulnerable people abandoned, and Burnham is right that mass child homelessness would be a disgrace. But that is not the actual choice in front of us. A serious country — one that cut the vast waste in Whitehall, controlled its borders, and got the economy growing — could fund a proper defence and a decent safety net. Presenting it as "the tank or the food bank" is a confession of failure dressed up as a value. The money is there; it is being wasted, not rationed between soldiers and the poor.

Where Reform UK Stands

National security comes first — not because we care less about the vulnerable, but because without it there is nothing to protect them with. Reform UK would fund our Armed Forces to meet the threats we actually face, pay for it by cutting the enormous waste in government rather than robbing either the military or the needy, and stop insulting the public with a false choice between defending the country and looking after its people. They are not opposites. They are the same duty. A Prime Minister who cannot see that has, quite literally, got his job the wrong way round.
