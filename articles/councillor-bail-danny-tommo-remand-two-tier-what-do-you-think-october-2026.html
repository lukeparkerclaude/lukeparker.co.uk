One Is Bailed Over Alleged Assaults on Police. Another Is Remanded Over a Boat. What Do You Make of That?

I am not going to tell you what to think about this. I am going to set out two cases, as reported, and ask you an honest question. In one, a councillor accused of assaulting three police officers is free on unconditional bail. In the other, an activist accused of damaging a boat is sitting in a cell. Both deny the charges and both deserve a fair trial. But side by side, does that look even-handed to you?

I want to do something different with this one. Rather than tell you what to think, I am going to lay out two cases exactly as they have been reported, ask an honest question, and then genuinely ask for your opinion. Because this is one of those moments where what the public feels really matters, and I would rather hear from you than lecture you.

Case One

This summer, a suspended local councillor in Birmingham was charged with five assault offences, including, according to the reporting, the alleged assault of three police officers, a member of the public and a member of police staff. She was granted unconditional bail and is due to stand trial next year.

Case Two

Around the same time, the activist known as Danny Tommo was remanded in custody after denying a charge of criminal damage, over the alleged slashing of a dinghy, with his trial set for next year. Bail was refused.

Fairness First, Because It Matters

Let me be completely clear, because it is right and because I mean it. Both of these people deny the charges against them. Both are entitled to the presumption of innocence, and to a fair trial. Neither has been convicted of anything. Bail and remand decisions are made by the courts on legal tests they are required to apply, including things like the risk of reoffending or of failing to attend court. In the Danny Tommo case the judge specifically said he was "not concerned with the political issues or motivations" and had set them aside. These are live cases. A court will decide them, not me and not a comment section, and I am not saying any individual decision was wrong.

But Here Is the Honest Question

Set the two side by side, as an ordinary member of the public inevitably will, and ask yourself honestly:

How does an alleged assault on three serving police officers result in unconditional bail, while an allegation of damaging a boat results in someone being held in a cell until trial? Yes, they are different charges and every case has its own detail. But can you see why, from the outside, a lot of people look at those two outcomes and ask whether we really do have one law for everyone? Nigel Farage and other senior figures have asked the same thing out loud. Right or wrong, that question is not going away.

Why It Matters

Justice does not only have to be done. It has to be seen to be done. The moment people start to feel that the seriousness of a charge, or the treatment of a defendant, depends on who they are or what they believe, faith in the whole system starts to drain away, and respect for the police who enforce the law drains with it. That confidence is hard won and very easily lost.

Over to You

So I am asking, genuinely. What do you make of it? Looking at those two cases side by side, does it feel even-handed to you? Do you think alleged assaults on police officers are treated with the seriousness they deserve? And do you believe the system applies the same rules to everyone, whatever their politics? Tell me honestly, because I would rather represent what you actually think than assume it. For my part, I will always back our police, and I will always argue for one law, applied equally to all. The rest, I want to hear from you.
