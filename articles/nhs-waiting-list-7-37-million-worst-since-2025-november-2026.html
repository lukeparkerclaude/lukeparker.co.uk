7.37 Million Treatments Waiting — and Only 65% Seen Within 18 Weeks

7.37 million treatments waiting at the end of August, up on July, and only around 65% treated within 18 weeks. Labour has pledged 92% by 2029. Let's be honest about the gap between that promise and this reality.

Here is the number: 7.37 million. That is how many treatments were waiting to be carried out at the end of August, relating to 6.24 million patients, according to the latest figures reported by Sky News. A month earlier it was 7.33 million treatments and 6.21 million patients. The list is going the wrong way.

Worst Since 2025

Sky's headline was that the waiting list is “at its worst since 2025”, and that it has risen “despite record levels of care being delivered”. I take the second half of that seriously: nurses and doctors are working flat out and I would never say otherwise. The problem is not the people at the front line. It is a system that keeps asking them to run faster on a treadmill that is speeding up.

The 18-Week Promise

The standard is that patients should start treatment within 18 weeks. Currently, around 65% are treated within that timeframe. The Government has pledged 92% by the end of this parliament in 2029.

That means closing a gap of roughly 27 percentage points in a little over two years — while the list is currently growing. My honest read: nobody watching these figures month after month believes that target is on track. Behind each of those numbers is somebody in pain, off work, or waiting for a scan that decides whether they live.

Waiting Isn't Free

People forget that a waiting list is not a queue in a shop. It is a household deciding whether to dip into savings for private care they can't really afford, a worker slipping off the payroll, a family watching a parent get worse. This is a cost-of-living story as much as a health story.

And social care is part of it. The Prime Minister announced a national care service in September, but according to Al Jazeera's Budget preview the implementation report is not due until summer 2027. That is a long way off for anyone stuck in a hospital bed waiting for a care package.

Where Reform UK Stands

Patients first, not process first. Reform UK's position is to cut the bureaucracy that sits between taxpayers' money and the front line, use spare capacity wherever it exists to bring waiting times down, and keep care free at the point of use. Fewer managers marking targets, more treatments delivered. That is not a slogan. It is the only way a 92% pledge ever becomes real.

Source: NHS waiting list (7.37m treatments / 6.24m patients end-August; 7.33m / 6.21m end-July; ~65% within 18 weeks; 92% pledge by 2029): Sky News, as republished by CIAC, 9 Oct 2026.
