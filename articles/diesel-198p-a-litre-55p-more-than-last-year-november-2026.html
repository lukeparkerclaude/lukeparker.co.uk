Diesel at 198p a Litre — 55p More Than This Time Last Year

The Government's own weekly figures put diesel at 198.19p a litre and petrol at 170.71p. A year ago they were 143.31p and 134.65p. Prices have eased a touch from October's peak — but for anyone who drives for a living, that is no comfort at all.

If you filled up this week and felt sick at the pump, the official numbers back you up. The Government's weekly road fuel prices for the week of 9 November 2026 put the average litre of diesel at 198.19p and petrol at 170.71p.

Twelve months ago, in the week of 10 November 2025, those same figures were 143.31p and 134.65p. That is diesel up nearly 55p a litre and petrol up 36p a litre in a single year.

What That Means for a Tank

Do the sums on a 55-litre tank. Diesel that cost about £78.82 to fill a year ago now costs about £109.00 — roughly £30 more every fill-up. A petrol tank has gone from about £74 to about £94.

To be fair, prices have come off the peak slightly. Diesel crossed 200p in the week of 12 October (200.04p) and petrol hit 175.28p that same week — its highest since August 2022. They have edged down since. But "slightly less awful than last month" is not a cost-of-living policy.

The Taxman's Share

Here is the bit Westminster would rather you didn't dwell on. Fuel duty is 52.95p a litre, and VAT at 20% is then charged on top — including on the duty. On a 198.19p litre of diesel, that VAT comes to about 33p. Put together, roughly 86p of every litre of diesel goes to the Treasury.

The Government will point out that it froze fuel duty in the Budget. Fine — I'll give them that. But when the pump price rises, the VAT take rises with it automatically. The Treasury gains from your pain without lifting a finger.

Who Really Pays

Let's be honest about who gets hit hardest. It isn't Whitehall. It's the delivery driver, the farmer, the builder with a van, the carer driving between visits, and the family in a rural village with no bus worth the name. Here in Lancashire, plenty of people have no choice but to drive — and diesel is what keeps vans, tractors and lorries moving. Every penny on diesel ends up in the price of everything else.

Where Reform UK Stands

Motorists are not a cash machine. Reform UK wants a lower tax burden on ordinary working people, and in my view that has to start at the pump — including ending the nonsense of charging VAT on top of duty, a tax on a tax. We also need to get serious about British energy production so we are less exposed to every shock in the Middle East. Cheaper fuel is not a luxury; it is the cheapest way to bring down the cost of almost everything.

Source: Department for Energy Security and Net Zero, 'Weekly road fuel prices' (CSV, 2018 onwards, published 10 November 2026): week commencing 9 November 2026 — ULSP 170.71p, ULSD 198.19p; week commencing 10 November 2025 — ULSP 134.65p, ULSD 143.31p; week commencing 12 October 2026 — ULSP 175.28p, ULSD 200.04p; duty 52.95p/litre, VAT 20%. Petrol 'highest since August 2022' per Brumble (4 November 2026). Tank costs (55 litres) and the tax-per-litre figure are calculated from these official numbers.
