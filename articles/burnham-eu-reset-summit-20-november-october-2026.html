'Britain Is Back', Says Burnham. Back Where, Exactly? The EU Summit Nobody Voted For

The Prime Minister has confirmed a UK-EU summit in Brussels on 20 November, with a youth experience scheme and immigration cooperation on the agenda. He promises that 'the British public will be the boss'. Let's look at what was actually said, and what the public hasn't been told.

On 12 October the Prime Minister, Andy Burnham, stood up in the Commons and told MPs: "Britain is back." He was reporting on the UN General Assembly and a long list of international business. Buried in it was the part that will matter most to your wallet and your borders.

What He Confirmed

According to the statement published on GOV.UK, the next UK-EU summit will take place in Brussels on 20 November. The topics he listed include a food and drink trade deal, emissions trading and energy links, a youth experience scheme, and immigration cooperation. He said the summit is expected to add the equivalent of over £10 billion a year to the UK economy by 2040, and he cited costs on the other side of the ledger too: paperwork that can add up to £1,400 per shipment for food producers, export health certificates costing up to £200 each, and a 100% rate of border document checks on Scottish salmon, costing around £100 million in lost exports.

Those are real frustrations for real businesses, and I will not pretend otherwise. If there are ways to cut pointless paperwork for British farmers and food producers without handing back control, nobody serious should be against it.

"The British Public Will Be the Boss"

The Prime Minister also said: "At every stage, the British public will be the boss." Fine words. But notice that the topics on the table include immigration cooperation and a youth scheme, and nobody has yet put a price or a number on either for the public to judge.

In 2016 Mr Burnham told the Commons that free movement had been "used to undermine skilled wages" and that "we did not do enough about it". He said the status quo of full free movement "was defeated at the ballot box". He was right then. So the question for him now is simple: what exactly is the youth scheme, how many people, for how long, and what does it cost?

Reform's Concerns, Clearly Labelled

Nigel Farage set out Reform's concerns in a piece dated 13 October. These are Reform's own estimates, and the piece does not cite its sources, so judge them accordingly: that rejoining could push net migration back up to about 500,000 a year, and that membership could cost up to £40 billion a year, compared with about £18 billion when the UK voted to leave in 2016. My honest read is that whatever the true number is, the public is entitled to see it before a deal is signed, not after.

Where Reform UK Stands

Trade with Europe, yes. Handing back control, no. Reform UK will back sensible measures that cut red tape for British exporters, but we will not accept a back-door route to free movement or open-ended payments to Brussels. Publish the youth scheme details, publish the costs, and if there is any move towards deeper integration, put it to the people. The British public cannot be the boss if they are the last to be told.
