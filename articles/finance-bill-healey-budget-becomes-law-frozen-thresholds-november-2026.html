Healey's Budget Is Becoming Law This Week. Here's What You're Actually Signing Up For

MPs debated the Finance Bill on Monday — the law that turns John Healey's 28 October Budget into reality. The headlines said 'no change to income tax rates'. The small print says frozen thresholds, a doubled bank surcharge and a forecast of no real growth in earnings next year.

Budgets get the headlines. Finance Bills get the law. And on Monday, while most people were getting on with their lives, MPs held the Second Reading of the Finance Bill that turns Chancellor John Healey's 28 October Budget into hard legislation.

So now the theatre is over, let's look at what is actually being written into law.

"No Change to Income Tax Rates" — But the Freeze Stays

The government's line is that the main rates of income tax, National Insurance and VAT are unchanged. That is true. It is also only half the story. The freeze on income tax thresholds announced in last year's Budget remains in place.

Let's be honest about what a threshold freeze is. When your wages rise just to keep up with prices but the tax bands don't move, more of your pay is taxed, and more of it at higher rates. You are not richer. The Treasury is. It is a tax rise that never has to be announced from the despatch box.

Even the Liberal Democrats have tabled an amendment opposing the threshold freeze. When the Lib Dems are to your right on taxing working people, something has gone badly wrong.

The Bank Surcharge Doubles — And Guess Who Pays

The Budget temporarily increases the Bank Corporation Tax Surcharge from 3% to 6% for 15 months, ending on 31 March 2028. It is expected to raise £1.5 billion, and the government says it will help fund household energy support.

My honest read: banks are not charities, and they do not absorb costs out of the goodness of their hearts. Higher taxes on lenders have a habit of turning up later in mortgage rates, account fees and tighter lending to small businesses. Help with energy bills is welcome — but funding it this way is a short-term fix, not a plan.

MPs will go through the income tax clauses and the bank surcharge line by line when the whole House sits as a committee on Monday 30 November. The High Value Council Tax Surcharge — the new levy on expensive homes in England, due to start in April 2028 — is scheduled for Tuesday 1 December.

The Forecast Nobody Wants to Talk About

Strip away the spin and look at the Office for Budget Responsibility's numbers. The OBR expects no growth in real earnings next year. It cut its growth forecast for 2027 to 1%, down from 1.5%. It forecasts household disposable income per person to fall by 0.6% in 2026/27.

That is the verdict of the government's own fiscal watchdog on Andy Burnham's first Budget: you will work just as hard, pay more of it in tax as thresholds stay frozen, and not be any better off.

Where Reform UK Stands

Working people should keep more of what they earn. Reform UK would lift the tax-free personal allowance substantially so that the lowest paid are taken out of income tax altogether, end the stealth tax of frozen thresholds, and pay for it by cutting waste — starting with the billions spent on asylum hotels and net zero subsidies — not by squeezing the same families year after year. A government that forecasts no real pay rise for anyone, then asks for more of your wages, has run out of ideas. We haven't.
