Just 1 in 4 Britons Believe Burnham Will Fix the Cost-of-Living Crisis

New Ipsos polling finds only 26% of Britons think Andy Burnham will change the country for the better — far below the optimism that greeted Keir Starmer in 2024. Three in four remain anxious about their own household finances.

Andy Burnham says he wants to lead a "cost of living government." The public, it turns out, isn't holding its breath. New Ipsos polling conducted between 21 and 23 July 2026, days into his premiership, shows a country that is bracing for more of the same rather than expecting relief.

The Numbers Behind the Anxiety

Seventy-six per cent of British adults say they are personally concerned about the impact of the rising cost of living over the next six months. That figure rises to 84% when people are asked about the cost of living for the country as a whole. Nearly seven in ten (68%) expect inflation to rise further over the next year, 47% are bracing for a drop in their disposable income, and 30% expect their overall standard of living to fall.

There is a sliver of good news buried in the detail: concern has eased slightly since its peak in March, in the aftermath of the closure of the Strait of Hormuz. But easing from a crisis point is not the same as recovery, and the numbers remain stubbornly high by any normal standard.

A Colder Welcome Than Starmer Got

The most telling figure in the whole survey is a comparison across time. Just 26% of Britons believe Andy Burnham will change the country for the better — compared with 43% who expected exactly that from Keir Starmer when he won the general election in July 2024. Only 28% currently hold a positive view of Burnham's premiership, against 21% negative and a striking 42% who simply don't know what to make of him yet.

On the specific question of managing the cost of living, only 22% expect his government to do a good job, with 23% expecting a bad one and the rest undecided. That is a public reserving judgement, not one offering a honeymoon.

Patience Is Not Infinite

Gideon Skinner, Senior Director of UK Politics at Ipsos, noted that while Burnham enters office without the negativity that had attached itself to Starmer by the end, "the events of the last couple of years have taken their toll on the public mood." Four in ten Britons think it is realistic to expect noticeable improvements within a year, but only half that number actually believe those improvements will come. Two-thirds think meaningful change should arrive within Labour's remaining three years in office — but again, only 44% think it actually will.

That gap between what people think is reasonable and what they expect to actually happen is the real story here. Labour has spent two years burning through public trust, and a change of leader in Downing Street has not been enough to buy back much of it.

What Reform UK Would Do

Reform UK's answer to the cost-of-living crisis has never been a rebrand at the top — it's lower taxes, a lighter regulatory burden on business, and an end to the net zero levies and stealth tax rises that have squeezed household budgets under Labour. Voters clearly aren't convinced a new face in Number 10 changes the underlying economics. Until the tax burden actually comes down, no amount of rebranding will shift a public mood this sceptical.

Source: Ipsos, "3 in 4 Britons anxious over personal cost of living as Andy Burnham takes office", published 24 July 2026. Fieldwork conducted 21–23 July 2026 among 1,050 adults.
