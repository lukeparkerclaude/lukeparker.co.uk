Channel Crossings Are Down 42%. Don't Let Labour Tell You It's Fixed.

Small boat arrivals are down 42% on last year, and I'll say so plainly. But 20,935 people is still a huge number, the Border Security Commander is warning of a 'mass casualty event', and asylum hotels are costing more per room, not less.

Let me start by being fair, because I want this argument to be bulletproof. Small boat crossings are down this year. If I said otherwise, I'd be wrong, and you'd be right to stop reading.

What the Figures Say

Between 1 January and 15 October 2026, 20,935 people crossed the Channel in small boats. That is 42% fewer than the 36,365 recorded over the same period in 2025, according to asylumstats.co.uk, which tracks the official figures. Arrivals are at their lowest point since 2020, according to the Border Security Commander.

But a fall is not a fix. Twenty thousand people is still twenty thousand people arriving by criminal gangs' boats in a single year.

The Warning Labour Should Be Listening To

UK Border Security Commander Duncan Capps told the Home Affairs Committee that it is "only a matter of time until we have a mass casualty event in the Channel." He said smugglers are adapting, with a record 230 people spotted on a single "mega dinghy" this year, and operations moving south towards Normandy, a crossing of nearly 60 nautical miles.

He also said around 3,800 migrants are in northern France, roughly double the figure of about two years ago. The UK and France have signed a three-year deal worth £660 million to stop the crossings. That is your money, and the test is results, not signatures.

The Hotel Bill

Credit where it is due: the number of people in asylum hotels has fallen, from 36,273 in September 2025 to 16,021 at the end of June 2026, according to Home Office data. But the average cost per person per night was £199 as of April, up 37% from £144.98 in October 2025. Home Office minister Anna Turley put that down to hotels still charging for rooms that are empty while closures are phased in.

Fewer people, higher price per head. Taxpayers are entitled to ask who negotiated that.

Where Reform UK Stands

A fall is welcome, but the aim is to stop the crossings, not manage them. That means a clear message that arriving illegally will not lead to a life in Britain, proper enforcement, and an end to paying over the odds for hotel rooms. Declare victory when the boats stop, not before.

Source: asylumstats.co.uk, 'How many small boats have crossed the Channel in 2026?' (20,935 to 15 Oct 2026 vs 36,365 same period 2025, -42%, published 16 Oct); The National, 21 Oct 2026 (Duncan Capps evidence to Home Affairs Committee: quote, 230 on one boat, Normandy route, c.3,800 in northern France, £660m UK-France deal, lowest since 2020); Home Office data via minister Anna Turley as reported by Breitbart, 23 Sep 2026 (hotel population 16,021 end June 2026 vs 36,273 Sep 2025; £199/night in April vs £144.98 Oct 2025).
