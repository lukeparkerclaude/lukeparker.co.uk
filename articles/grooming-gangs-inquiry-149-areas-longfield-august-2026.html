149 Areas, 250,000 Victims: The Grooming Gangs Report Ministers Can’t Ignore

A privately-funded inquiry led by Rupert Lowe has found evidence of organised grooming gang activity in at least 149 local authority areas, with claims the true number of victims could exceed 250,000. The official government inquiry, chaired by Baroness Longfield, has barely begun.

It took a privately-funded inquiry, not the British state, to put a number on the scale of Britain’s grooming gangs scandal. The Rape Gang Inquiry Report, commissioned by Restore Britain and led by MP Rupert Lowe, concludes that organised child sexual exploitation networks operated in at least 149 local authority areas across the United Kingdom, with the true number of victims potentially exceeding 250,000.

A Report the State Should Have Written Itself

This is not the official government inquiry. It is a 219-page report compiled over months of hearings, survivor testimony, whistleblower accounts and court records — assembled by a privately organised effort because, for years, the institutions responsible for protecting children did not do this work themselves. It has no statutory powers and could not compel witnesses or evidence. That it nonetheless amounts to one of the most comprehensive public accounts of this scandal yet produced says a great deal about the scale of institutional failure that came before it.

The report’s figures have not been independently verified and its methodology has been questioned by some commentators, given its non-statutory, campaign-funded origins. But survivor testimony, whistleblower accounts and court records assembled across 149 separate local authority areas describe a pattern that goes well beyond a handful of isolated local failures, and it deserves serious, urgent scrutiny rather than dismissal.

Where Is the Official Inquiry?

Britain does have an official, government-established Independent Inquiry into Grooming Gangs, chaired by Baroness Anne Longfield, the former Children’s Commissioner for England. It was formally set up on 13 April 2026, with a £65 million budget and up to three years to report, covering England and Wales. Nearly four months after that starting gun, with a privately-funded report now setting out claims of national scale, the public is entitled to ask what the statutory inquiry has actually done with the time and money it has been given.

Ethnicity and Culture Must Not Be Off Limits

Both the Restore Britain report and the official inquiry’s own terms of reference confirm they will examine the role that ethnicity, religion and culture played in these crimes, rather than treating that question as too sensitive to ask. Previous local reviews were repeatedly criticised for downplaying or avoiding this question out of fear of the reaction it might provoke. Survivors paid the price for that institutional caution. Any inquiry, official or otherwise, that is prepared to follow the evidence wherever it leads is the minimum victims are owed.

What Reform UK Would Do

Reform UK has consistently called for a full, transparent, statutory investigation of grooming gangs, with no institution and no question treated as off-limits. Baroness Longfield’s inquiry should treat the Restore Britain report’s findings as a starting point for urgent statutory investigation, not something to be quietly reviewed on a three-year timetable. Victims who have already waited a decade or more for accountability should not be asked to wait until 2029 to find out whether the claims in this report hold up.

Sources: Rape Gang Inquiry Report, commissioned by Restore Britain and led by MP Rupert Lowe, published June 2026 (IBTimes UK; The European Conservative); House of Commons Library, briefing on the official Independent Inquiry into Grooming Gangs (CBP-10613); Hansard, Grooming Gangs Independent Inquiry debate, 13 April 2026.
