200 Charities Tell Burnham to Scrap Border Laws While Channel Crossings Hit Record Highs

Over 200 asylum and migrant support organisations have written to the new Prime Minister demanding he repeal the Illegal Migration Act, scrap detention and end the six-month ban on asylum seekers working. This is the opposite of what the country needs.

While small boat crossings run at record levels for the year, the asylum lobby has chosen this moment to demand the government go further in the other direction entirely. More than 200 organisations and 289 individuals, coordinated by the campaign group Asylum Matters, have written an open letter to Prime Minister Andy Burnham calling for what they term a "blueprint to fix the broken asylum system." Read the small print, and it amounts to a demand for open borders dressed up in the language of compassion.

What They Are Actually Asking For

The letter, signed by organisations including Doctors of the World UK, Freedom from Torture and JCWI, sets out 13 recommendations. Among them: restoring and expanding "safe legal routes" to the UK, ending the ban on asylum seekers working after just six months, increasing legal aid funding, and replacing immigration detention with "community-based alternatives." It goes further still, calling on the government to scrap the current Asylum and Immigration Bill entirely and fully repeal both the Illegal Migration Act 2023 and the Nationality and Borders Act 2022 — the two pieces of legislation that give the state any meaningful power to remove people with no right to be here.

The letter also demands the government abandon plans for a proposed £10,000 "refugee tax," the expansion of ex-military accommodation sites, and reforms to refugee settlement rules. Nathan Phillips of Asylum Matters said the group's blueprint was "based on real evidence" and that it was "perfectly possible to build an asylum system with welcome at its heart that is both efficient and cost effective." The organisations describe current policy as "performative cruelty that fails on its own terms."

A System That Needs Control, Not More Openness

Here is what that framing leaves out: the reason public confidence in the asylum system has collapsed is not because Britain has been too tough. It is because, for years, the system has failed to remove people with no right to be here, has struggled to process claims in any reasonable timeframe, and has allowed record numbers of small boat arrivals year after year. Scrapping the laws that allow removals and ending work restrictions would not fix that failure — it would guarantee it continues indefinitely.

Ending the six-month work ban in particular would remove one of the only remaining incentives for asylum claims to be processed quickly. If someone can work regardless of whether their claim is genuine or has even been assessed, the pressure to resolve cases fairly and fast disappears. That is not a fix. That is surrender dressed up as reform.

Burnham's Response Tells Its Own Story

Tellingly, the Prime Minister has not rushed to embrace this agenda. Burnham last week reappointed Shabana Mahmood as Home Secretary, signalling no immediate change of direction on asylum policy. Mahmood said she intended to "finish the job" of what she called major reform to make Britain's streets safer and the immigration system "fair for all." Whether that holds under sustained pressure from more than 200 well-organised campaign groups remains to be seen — but the letter shows exactly where the pressure on this government is coming from, and it is not from voters who want tougher borders.

What Reform UK Would Do

Reform UK's position is straightforward: asylum claims should be processed within months, not years, genuine refugees should be supported, and those with no right to remain should be removed swiftly. That means keeping, not repealing, the legal framework that allows removals. It means resisting calls to grant work rights before status is settled. And it means recognising that public trust in the asylum system will only be rebuilt by demonstrating control, not by outsourcing policy to campaign groups who have never had to answer to the electorate for the consequences of open borders.

Source: Electronic Immigration Network, "Over 200 organisations call for new direction on asylum policy in open letter to Prime Minister Andy Burnham," 29 July 2026.
