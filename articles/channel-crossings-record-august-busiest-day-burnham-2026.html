Record Month, Record Day: Channel Crossings Surge as Burnham's Border Crackdown Unravels

More than 1,200 migrants have crossed the Channel in the first days of August alone — a record for the month — with Wednesday's 752 arrivals the busiest single day of the year. Andy Burnham's promised crackdown is nowhere to be seen.

Four days into August, and the small boats crisis has already written a new record. More than 1,200 people have crossed the Channel so far this month, the highest total for the opening days of August on record, according to reporting on Home Office arrival figures. Wednesday alone saw 752 arrivals — the busiest single day for crossings anywhere in 2026. This is what Andy Burnham's government calls a crackdown.

2,057 Arrivals Since Burnham Took Over

Andy Burnham became Prime Minister on 20 July, promising a fresh start after Labour's chaotic leadership contest. Since then, 2,057 people have arrived by small boat, including 326 in four boats on a single Saturday. Two and a half weeks in charge, and the Channel is busier than at almost any point this year. If this was meant to be a reset on border policy, nobody told the people-smugglers.

Reform UK has said all along that changing the Labour leader was never going to change Labour's approach to the border. Burnham inherits the same asylum system, the same processing backlog, and the same reluctance to remove people with no right to be here. A new face in Downing Street doesn't stop a single boat.

The Numbers Labour Doesn't Want You to Read Carefully

To be fair to the government, the provisional total for the year so far — around 14,526 — is lower than the same point in both 2025 (25,436) and 2024 (16,903). Ministers will point to that as proof their approach is working. But a lower yearly average means little when a single month can set a new record on its own. Crossings are seasonal, weather-dependent, and driven by the smuggling gangs' operating capacity — not by government press releases. August, historically one of the busiest months, has started exactly as badly as ever.

The government's flagship response is a £662 million payment to France, running from 2026/27 to 2028/29, to "strengthen operations" against crossings in northern France. Reform UK's position is simple: Britain has now paid France hundreds of millions of pounds across successive deals, and the boats keep coming. Paying a neighbouring government to patrol its own beaches was never going to be a substitute for a returns policy that actually removes people who arrive here illegally.

What Reform UK Would Do

Reform UK would end the small boats crisis by removing the incentive to make the crossing in the first place: anyone arriving illegally would be detained and swiftly returned, not processed into the asylum system and dispersed into hotels and communities while their claim drags on for years. We'd redirect the money currently spent on Channel patrol contracts with France into a proper detention and removals capability of our own. And we'd stop pretending that a change of Labour leader is a change of policy — because on the evidence of the last fortnight, it plainly isn't.

Every record month is a fresh reminder that Britain's borders are not under control. Preston East residents don't need another press conference about a deal with France. They need boats stopped and people who arrive illegally removed.

Sources: ITV News, "Number of people crossing in small boats tops 2,000 since Andy Burnham became PM" (2 August 2026); House of Commons Library, Statistics on small boat Channel crossings; reporting on 2026 provisional Channel crossing totals and the UK-France £662 million migration agreement (2026/27–2028/29).
